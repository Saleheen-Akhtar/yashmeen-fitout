# Customer Psychology

## Primary decision-makers

### Luxury residential client

Needs a finished interior with strong craft, predictable coordination and minimal personal involvement. Fears cost drift, poor workmanship, delays and a designer/contractor blame gap. Trusts a clear process, workshop evidence, relevant villa work and a named accountable contact.

### Commercial office client

Needs a compliant, coordinated workplace delivered around operational deadlines. Fears disruption, authority delays, MEP rework and unclear responsibility. Trusts approvals expertise, sequencing, programme clarity and direct project proof.

### Hospitality/F&B operator

Needs a distinctive guest environment delivered against an opening date. Fears late handover, coordination failure and finish quality under pressure. Trusts parallel workshop/site production, material control and a clear handover path.

## Ranked trust signals

1. Specific completed projects with scope, location and images.
2. One-team accountability explained in operational terms.
3. In-house Al Quoz workshop and visible craft.
4. Authority approvals and MEP coordination explained clearly.
5. Response promise and direct contact details.

## Conversion wins

- Put the quote CTA beside the first proof of accountability.
- Ask for only name, mobile, email, service and project details initially.
- Tell visitors what happens next: review, reply within one working day, then scope discussion.
- Use service-specific project examples instead of broad adjectives.
